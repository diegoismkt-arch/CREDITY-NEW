# Retention & virality drivers in short vertical video (FB Reels, TikTok, Shorts, IG Reels) — applied to Spanish-language AI mini-telenovelas (20-90 s, Facebook-first)

> Research note (Oct 2026). Method caveat: direct page fetches failed (DNS errors on emplifi.io, about.fb.com, socialmediatoday.com, wharton.upenn.edu), so most findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages. Primary sources are flagged where known; vendor/blog claims are labelled as such. **[HARD DATA]** = study/platform figure with known sample or primary origin; **[PLATFORM]** = statement by Meta/TikTok/YouTube (often reported secondhand); **[OPINION/BLOG]** = practitioner claim, not verified.

## 1. The first 1-3 seconds: drop-off and hook types

### Takeaway
The only sizeable Facebook-specific dataset (Emplifi, 10,110 FB Reels) says what wins the opening is **human speech + a visible person + vertical framing**, not frantic pacing. Platforms (Meta, YouTube) explicitly say the opening seconds decide whether people keep watching, and give creators a metric for it (YouTube "viewed vs. swiped away"), but no platform publishes a target drop-off number. Many widely quoted "X% in 3 seconds" stats are misattributed.

### Cited Findings
- **[HARD DATA, Facebook]** Emplifi analysed 10,110 Facebook Reels from 704 brand Pages: Reels with **human speech in the first 3 seconds** performed better; speech-led openings beat music-only openings with **+24.7% 10-second retention**. — [Emplifi, Facebook Reels data](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/); [Social Media Today coverage](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/human-speech-and-presence-help-drive-reels-engagement/815381)
- **[HARD DATA, Facebook]** Same study: a **visible person early on** correlated with better retention; **vertical videos +9.1% 3-second retention and +23% 10-second retention**; **pacing (cut speed) showed minimal impact on retention**. — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/) (as summarized in search results; brand Pages only, may not transfer exactly to creator pages)
- **[PLATFORM, Meta]** Meta's Reels guidance (marketing/creator playbooks): "nail the hook" in the opening seconds; recommended hook styles = lead with the viewer's benefit, state upfront what the video will show, or ask a question inviting participation; A/B test hooks; use music or voiceover; dynamic editing (zooms, transitions, short cuts); feature real people; 9:16, sound-on, respect safe zones. — [Social Media Today: Meta shares tips on Reels hooks](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182); [onlinemarketing.de on Meta Reels guide](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/meta-leidfaden-tipps-insights-herausragende-reels); [House of Marketers, Meta Reels Performance Playbook summary](https://houseofmarketers.com/meta-reels-marketing-tips-playbook/)
- **[PLATFORM-ish, ads]** Meta-reported figure: Reel ads with human presence had **27% higher CTR** (ad context, not organic). — [logie.ai](https://logie.ai/?p=5172)
- **[PLATFORM, YouTube]** YouTube's Todd Sherman: Shorts are judged differently from long-form because viewers don't choose them, they scroll a feed; not every swipe is a view. YouTube Studio exposes **"Viewed vs. swiped away"** (share of feed impressions that became a view); Sherman's advice: compare it across your own Shorts to learn which openings hold people. YouTube publishes **no target** value. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/); [Outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[PLATFORM, YouTube]** Shorts are first shown to a small seed audience, then expanded or stopped based on that group's response; early views can taper while YouTube searches for the right audience. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/fr/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[MYTH-BUSTING]** The popular "63% of top TikTok ads hook in 3 seconds" stat is a misreading: TikTok's ad guide said that among highest-CTR videos the key message/product appears early; no sample, date or method given. A "65% of viewers… TikTok internal research" stat and "45% who watch 3 s continue 30 s more (Facebook)" have no primary source found. — [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/); [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/calculators/watch-time/tiktok-hook-strategies/)
- **[BENCHMARK, vendor]** "Hook rate" = 3-second views ÷ impressions ×100. Typical creator hook rates 20–35% (one vendor); short-form ad average ~25–30% (Motion). — [posteverywhere.ai](https://posteverywhere.ai/social-media-terms/hook-rate); [nestscale](https://nestscale.com/?p=47200); [Tuff Growth](https://tuffgrowth.com/benchmarking-tiktok-video-ad-hooks)
- **[PLATFORM, Instagram/Meta, secondhand]** Instagram's AI reportedly predicts likelihood a viewer stays ≥3 seconds. — [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)
- **[OPINION/BLOG]** "If someone scrolls before 3 s the platform reads it as not engaging" — common creator claim, not confirmed by Meta. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/why-your-facebook-reels-get-no-views/); [Opus Clip hook formulas](https://www.opus.pro/blog/facebook-reels-hook-formulas)

### Inferences
- For AI telenovelas: open **mid-scene with a character speaking a conflict line on camera** (face visible, lip-synced dialogue, e.g. "¿Cómo que estás embarazada de mi esposo?") rather than an establishing shot or music intro. This matches the strongest FB-specific data (speech + person) better than "visual shock" alone.
- Fast cutting is not where to spend effort (Emplifi: pacing minimal impact); spend it on the first line of dialogue and the first frame.
- Conflict/accusation openings are a hybrid of Meta's "state what the video will show" and the curiosity gap (section 5). Visual shock (slap, scream, thrown drink) works best combined with a spoken line.
- Use Facebook Insights' retention graph (and, if cross-posting, YouTube's viewed-vs-swiped) to A/B test hooks: same episode, two different first lines.

### Gaps
- No published Meta figure for average 3-second drop-off on Facebook Reels, nor a "good" threshold.
- No controlled study comparing hook types (action vs. question vs. conflict vs. visual shock) in scripted drama; only vendor/blog formulas.
- Emplifi absolute retention baselines (not just relative lifts) were not retrievable.

## 2. Optimal length, watch time, and how Facebook ranks Reels (+ monetization)

### Takeaway
Across platforms, watch time/retention and "sends"(shares) are the top-weighted signals; Facebook now treats all video as Reels. Length evidence is mixed: very short (7–15 s) wins on completion %, while 30–90 s can win on total watch time and views *if the narrative holds*. Facebook monetization (Content Monetization, invite-only) pays on "Qualified Views" and explicitly favours **original** content that keeps people watching; repeated unoriginal reposting is penalised.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM, Facebook]** Mid-2025 Meta reclassified all videos on Facebook as Reels (short and long in one distribution system). — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/facebook-algorithm); [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/facebook-algorithm)
- **[PLATFORM, Facebook, secondhand]** Meta reported US video watch time on Facebook grew >20% YoY. — [Teleprompter stats](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/facebook-video-statistics); [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/?p=11020)
- **[BLOG, Facebook]** Reels ranking reportedly weighs how fast the video captures attention, total watch time and especially completion; loops/rewatches boost ranking; shares and saves weigh more than likes; Reels lean on discovery (non-followers) vs. Feed on relationships; watermarked (e.g., TikTok) reposts penalised. — [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/facebook-algorithm); [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/facebook-algorithm); [Jeff Bullas forum](https://www.jeffbullas.com/thread/how-does-facebooks-algorithm-for-reels-differ-from-the-main-news-feed-in-2025/). Unverified numbers in these sources (e.g., "70% completion threshold", "Reels = 38.4% of FB time Q1 2026") lack Meta backing.
- **[PLATFORM, Instagram — sister system]** Adam Mosseri (Jan 2025, secondhand) named the top 3 ranking signals: **watch time**, **likes per reach** (matters more for followers), **sends per reach** (matters most for reaching non-followers; "one of the biggest signals"). The "sends = 3–5× likes" figure is NOT clearly from Mosseri. — [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers); [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/how-does-the-algorithm-for-reels-work-in-2025/)
- **[PLATFORM, YouTube]** From 31 Mar 2025 a Short view counts on every start/loop (no minimum); "engaged views" are separate and are what count for YPP eligibility/revenue. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[VENDOR DATA, length]** Analysis of 500 viral Reels (Oct 2024–Mar 2025): 7–15 s had highest average completion; 16–30 s also strong; completion dropped noticeably past 30 s **unless the content had a clear narrative or transformation**. Analyst not named. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/video-length-sweet-spots-tiktok-reels-shorts); [Klap](https://klap.app/blog/how-long-should-reels-be)
- **[CONFLICTING, length]** Other sources claim 60–90 s Reels had highest average views/engagement; a "Meta data shows 30–90 s outperform" claim has no locatable source document. — [go-viral.app](https://www.go-viral.app/blog/instagram-reels-length/); [Klap](https://klap.app/blog/how-long-should-reels-be)
- **[OPINION/BLOG, Facebook]** First cut 15–30 s unless story needs more; how-to 30–45 s. — [Opus Clip, ideal FB Reels length](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-facebook-reels-length-format-retention)
- **[HARD DATA, Facebook text overlays/length interplay]** Emplifi: on music-led clips, text overlays raised retention up to 9.8% at the 30-second mark. — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **[BENCHMARK, Facebook engagement]** Socialinsider: average Facebook engagement rate ~0.15%; status posts led at 0.20% in 2025; in Q1 2026 Reels were the only format whose engagement rose. — [Socialinsider FB benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/facebook); [Socialinsider ES](https://www.socialinsider.io/es/social-media-benchmarks/facebook)
- **[PLATFORM, Facebook monetization]** Content Monetization is invite-only (express interest via Professional Dashboard); performance-based pay across Reels, longer video, Stories, photo/text; Meta weights **original content that keeps people watching and gets meaningful engagement**. Metrics: **Qualified Views**, **Earnings Rate** (≈ earnings per 1,000 qualified views), Non-Qualified Views; no public numeric cutoff. — [Buffer, make money on Facebook 2026](https://www.buffer.com/resources/how-to-make-money-on-facebook/); [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/facebook-announces-new-monetization-program-for-creators)
- **[PLATFORM, Facebook]** Meta: in 2025 Facebook paid creators nearly **$3 billion**; **Reels = 60% of creator payouts**. Creator Fast Track (Mar 2026): guaranteed $1,000/mo (≥100k followers elsewhere) or $3,000/mo (>1M) for 3 months; requires 15 Reels in 30 days over ≥10 different days; US/Canada only (so not directly available to MX/LatAm creators). — [Meta Newsroom, Creator Fast Track](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/creator-fast-track-grow-your-audience-earn-money-on-facebook/); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/facebook-launches-creator-fast-track-program-as-platform-reports-3b-usd-in-creator-payouts-for-2025/); [Buffer](https://www.buffer.com/resources/how-to-make-money-on-facebook/)
- **[PLATFORM, Facebook originality]** July 2025: accounts that repeatedly reuse others' videos/photos/text lose monetization for a period and get reduced distribution on all posts; minor edits (watermarks, stitched clips) are not "meaningful enhancement"; duplicate-detection deployed. H1 2025: ~10M impersonator profiles removed, ~500k accounts penalised for spam/fake engagement. No Meta rule singles out AI video per se. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/facebook-push-against-unoriginal-content/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/after-youtube-meta-crackdowns-on-unoriginal-content-removes-10-million-fake-facebook-profiles-74532.htm)

### Inferences
- For 20–90 s episodes: a 25–45 s episode that ends on a cliffhanger likely balances completion % and total watch time; go to 60–90 s only when each 10–15 s beat adds a new reveal (the "clear narrative" exception).
- Loop design: ending that cuts back to/echoes the opening line encourages rewatches (counted as watch time/loops).
- Originality matters for monetization: each AI episode must be genuinely new (own characters/scripts); avoid reposting the same clip re-cut, watermarks from other apps, or near-duplicate templates across pages — Meta's duplicate detection could flag repetitive AI templates.
- Shares ("sends") are the lever for reaching non-followers; design episodes people send to a friend/partner ("mira, igualita a tu suegra").

### Gaps
- No primary Meta document with Reels ranking weights; Meta Transparency Center "Reels" system card not retrieved.
- No public Qualified View definition (minimum seconds) or RPM ranges for Mexico/LatAm.
- No length-vs-completion data specific to scripted drama.

## 3. What triggers comments and shares (open loops, controversy, "Part 2" CTAs, identification)

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed social-media research shows **moral-emotional language** and **out-group animosity** ("them vs. us", villains) strongly raise sharing; high-arousal emotions drive transmission. However, Facebook has demoted **engagement bait** (incl. comment-baiting like "comment YES") since 2017 — so "comenta PARTE 2" is a risk, not a proven win. No hard data was found on "comment Part 2" CTAs or free serialized "Part 2" performance.

### Cited Findings
- **[HARD DATA]** Brady et al. 2017 (PNAS, n=563,312 posts): each additional **moral-emotional word** raised diffusion ~20% ("moral contagion"); stronger within ideological in-groups. 2025 pre-registered replication (N=849,266) found smaller effect (IRR 1.15 vs 1.20); meta-analytic estimate **~12% more sharing per moral-emotional word**. — [CSMaP NYU](https://csmapnyu.org/research/academic-research/emotion-shapes-the-diffusion-of-moralized-content-in-social-networks); [Kellogg 2025 effect-size paper](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2025/estimating-the-effect-size-of-moral-contagion-in-online/)
- **[HARD DATA]** Rathje, Van Bavel & van der Linden 2021 (PNAS, n≈2.73M posts on Facebook & Twitter): posts about the out-group much more likely shared; each out-group word raised odds of sharing ~67% (one summary) — another summary says 35–57%; effect ~4.8× negative-affect language and ~6.7× moral-emotional language. — [PMC full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8256037/); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/hostility-towards-outsiders-motivates-engagement-on-social-media); [Deric Bownds summary](https://mindblog.dericbownds.net/2021/07/out-group-animosity-drives-engagement.html) (political news context, not fiction)
- **[PLATFORM, Facebook]** Dec 2017: Facebook demotes posts using "engagement bait" (vote-baiting, react-baiting, share-baiting, tag-baiting, comment-baiting); ML model trained on hundreds of thousands of posts; stricter Page-level demotions for systematic use; expanded to many languages in 2018. Exempt: genuine requests for help/advice/recommendations. — [Meta Newsroom 2017](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/); [CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2017/12/18/technology/facebook-spam-posts/index.html); [Revive Social](https://revive.social/facebook-engagement-bait/)
- **[PLATFORM, Meta]** Meta's Reels hook guidance includes "ask a question that invites participation" — i.e., genuine questions are endorsed, mechanical bait is not. — [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)
- **[PLATFORM, TikTok]** TikTok "Series" = paywalled episodic collections (up to 80 videos, ≤20 min each, $1–190); paywalled clips don't appear in recommendations. No engagement data on cliffhangers published. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/07/tiktok-expands-series-paywall-feature-to-more-creators/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/tiktoks-series-feature-will-allow-creators-to-charge-for-premium-content-140033561.html)
- **[MARKET DATA, LatAm microdramas]** Omdia: Brazil 24M and **Mexico 20M monthly microdrama users (2025)**; global microdrama revenue ~$11B 2025 → >$14B 2026 → >$22B 2030; expects Spanish/Portuguese demand to accelerate. Sensor Tower: LatAm short-drama app downloads +69% QoQ in Q1 2025 (~100M); ReelShort >450 titles in Mexico. Producers credit telenovelas as groundwork for cliffhanger-driven format. — [TodoTV News / Omdia](https://todotvnews.com/en/omdia-brazil-and-mexico-drive-latin-americas-microdrama-growth/); [Señal News MIPCOM 2025](https://senalnews.com/en/interviews/mipcom-2025-short-vertical-content-the-new-language-of-young-and-not-so-young-generations); [Señal News May 2026](https://senalnews.com/en/data/brazil-and-mexico-rank-among-the-worlds-largest-microdrama-markets-outside-china/amp)

### Inferences
- Comments come most naturally from **moral judgment prompts embedded in the story**: a clear villain (suegra, amante, esposo infiel, patrón abusivo) and an injustice → viewers take sides ("¿Quién tiene la culpa?"). Out-group/villain framing is the fiction analogue of Rathje's out-group effect.
- Prefer a genuine, story-specific question ("¿Tú la perdonarías?") over mechanical "comenta PARTE 2 / escribe SÍ", which matches Facebook's comment-bait classifier pattern. If a Part-2 CTA is used, make it informational ("La parte 2 ya está en mi perfil / mañana a las 8") rather than conditioning release on comments.
- Serialization works as a market (Omdia/Sensor Tower), but the evidence for *free* Part-2 Reels driving follows is anecdotal; test it with Insights (follows per episode, profile visits).
- Identification triggers sharing to specific people (tagging/sending to a sister, a friend with a cheating ex) — aligns with "sends per reach".

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence found on "comment PART 2" CTAs (lift or penalty) on Facebook in 2024–2026; current Meta wording of comment-bait demotion not retrieved (only 2017).
- No data on whether serialized Part 1/Part 2 Reels increase follow rate vs. standalone episodes.
- No study on comment drivers specific to fiction/drama.

## 4. Captions/subtitles, sound-off viewing, text overlays, first frame

### Takeaway
Sound-off viewing is common (survey: 69% in public places), captions raise stated completion intent, and on Facebook text overlays measurably lift retention for music-led clips. But the Emplifi data also show that *spoken* dialogue is the strongest opener — so the right move is burned-in Spanish captions on top of spoken dialogue, not text instead of speech. The often-quoted "85% of Facebook video watched muted" is weakly sourced and dates from the pre-Reels era.

### Cited Findings
- **[HARD DATA, survey]** Verizon Media/Publicis survey, 5,616 US adults 18–54: **69% watch video with sound off in public**; 25% sound off even in private; **80% more likely to watch an entire video when captions are available**; for mobile, captions → +8% ad recall, +10% ad memory quality. — [3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/); [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)
- **[WEAK/UNVERIFIED]** "85% of Facebook videos watched without sound" (attributed to Digiday, old, pre-Reels); "75% of mobile views sound off" (HubSpot); "captions +12% view time on Facebook video ads". — [beey.io](https://www.beey.io/en/captions-increase-viewership-accessibility-and-reach-23486/); [testparty](https://testparty.ai/blog/media-accessibility-statistics); [posteverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/social-media-terms/sound-on-vs-sound-off)
- **[HARD DATA, Facebook]** Emplifi: text overlays up to **+9.8% retention at 30 s** on music-led Reels; speech openings +24.7% 10-s retention. — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **[PLATFORM, Meta]** Meta recommends sound-on creative and respecting safe zones (UI covers top/bottom/right). — [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/meta-reels-marketing-tips-playbook/)

### Inferences
- Burn in large, high-contrast Spanish captions of every line, kept inside the central safe zone; captions double as the hook for muted scrollers.
- Frame 1 = face mid-emotion + a short text hook (e.g., "Descubrió la verdad en su boda") so both muted and unmuted viewers get the conflict instantly.
- Facebook audience skews older in LatAm (inference, not sourced here); large captions also help readability.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 Facebook-specific sound-off rate for Reels; no data on thumbnail/cover effect for Reels distributed in-feed (covers matter mainly on profile grids).

## 5. Psychology: curiosity gap, Zeigarnik, high-arousal emotion, social currency

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced lever is **high-arousal emotion** (Berger & Milkman: awe, anger, anxiety boost sharing; sadness suppresses it). Curiosity gap (Loewenstein) is a solid theory but has thin direct evidence for video; the Zeigarnik effect has mixed replications, so "cliffhangers work because of Zeigarnik" is a plausible hypothesis, not established science.

### Cited Findings
- **[HARD DATA]** Berger & Milkman (Journal of Marketing Research, 2012), all NYT articles over ~3 months + lab experiments: positive content more viral than negative overall, but **arousal** drives it — **awe** (positive) and **anger, anxiety** (negative) increase sharing; **sadness** (low arousal) decreases it; holds controlling for surprise, interest, practical value and placement; experiments show the causal effect runs through activation. — [Berger & Milkman PDF](https://qubeshub.org/app/site/collections/4299/Berger__Milkman.pdf); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral); [Ragan](https://www.ragan.com/study-positive-content-most-likely-to-go-viral/)
- **[THEORY]** Loewenstein (1994) information-gap theory: curiosity arises from a perceived gap between what one knows and wants to know; requires some initial knowledge; gaps that feel too large or too small fail. — [Time Ideas](https://ideas.time.com/?p=31311); [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/create-ads-that-stick); [Boris Smus](https://smus.com/books/psychology-of-curiosity/)
- **[THEORY, mixed evidence]** Zeigarnik (1927): interrupted tasks recalled ~2× better than completed ones; later replications are mixed; no controlled studies link Zeigarnik to binge/cliffhanger retention. — [Learnnovators](https://learnnovators.com/?p=23026); [Autonolab blog](https://blog.autonolab.com/blog/2025-08-22-open-loops-and-curiosity-gaps-the-psychology-of-binge-watching/)
- **[HARD DATA, related]** Moral-emotional language (Brady) and out-group animosity (Rathje) raise sharing — see section 3.

### Inferences
- Script each episode around one high-arousal emotion: **anger/indignation** (injustice, betrayal, humiliation of the protagonist) or **awe/triumph** (karma, the humble girl revealed as heiress, the villain exposed). Avoid ending on pure sadness (low arousal) — end on outrage or anticipation.
- Curiosity gap: give enough context in the hook to make the gap "closable" (who, what's at stake) then withhold the resolution until the last 2 s or the next episode.
- Social currency (Berger's STEPPS, from his book *Contagious* — not retrieved here): shares happen when sharing says something about the sharer (moral stance, "esto me pasó"). The comment section becomes a moral-debate arena.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study on cliffhanger effects in short vertical video; no effect sizes retrieved from Berger & Milkman (fetch failed); STEPPS framework not sourced in this session.
